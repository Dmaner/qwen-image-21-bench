# C1: Qwen-Image 2.1 BF16 versus FP8 quality, memory and latency (H200)

For [vllm-project/vllm-omni#8586](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-omni/issues/8586). The primary comparison is BF16 versus DiT-only FP8; Text Encoder FP8 and combined DiT + Text Encoder FP8 are additional configurations. All results below use the same H200 and matched inputs/settings from the 2026-10-11 run.

## 1. Latency

Offline wall time per image, n=10. The timer covers `omni.generate(...)` and output processing, excluding initialization, warmup and PNG saving.

| Configuration | T2I | Δ vs BF16 | Edit (1 ref) | Δ vs BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BF16 | 7.260 ± 0.016 s | — | 8.332 ± 0.074 s | — |
| DiT FP8 | 6.018 ± 0.013 s | −17.1% | 7.109 ± 0.081 s | −14.7% |
| Text Encoder FP8 | 7.284 ± 0.021 s | +0.3% | 8.376 ± 0.081 s | +0.5% |
| DiT + Text Encoder FP8 | 6.013 ± 0.009 s | −17.2% | 7.078 ± 0.062 s | −15.0% |

DiT-only FP8 reduces T2I latency by **17.1%** and editing latency by **14.7%**. Text Encoder FP8 provides no latency benefit on these inputs. All **80 measured requests** across the four configurations and two tasks completed without recorded errors or OOMs.

The reduction is concentrated in denoising. Mean `diffuse` time for all 50 steps, from the same requests:

| Task | BF16 | DiT FP8 | Δ vs BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| T2I | 7.054 s | 5.806 s | −17.7% |
| Edit (1 ref) | 7.970 s | 6.701 s | −15.9% |

[Latency and stage measurements](../data/phase2/performance/PERFORMANCE.json).

## 3. Image Quality

Each FP8 configuration is paired with BF16 using identical prompts, seeds and reference inputs. The initial set contains four prompts × seeds 42/43/44; the held-out set contains four additional prompts × seeds 45/46/47, fixed before evaluation. The cases cover ordinary T2I, English/Chinese text and single-reference editing.

The mean scores combine all 24 paired cases (12 initial + 12 held-out). All cases completed without inference errors.

| Configuration vs BF16 | Mean PSNR ↑ | Mean SSIM ↑ | Mean LPIPS ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DiT FP8 | 25.41 dB | 0.9357 | 0.0748 |
| Text Encoder FP8 | 26.10 dB | 0.9319 | 0.0728 |
| DiT + Text Encoder FP8 | 23.42 dB | 0.9097 | 0.0951 |

### Examples with larger differences

The following text prompts show larger changes than the teapot example. Each pair uses the same prompt and seed; the images are original experiment outputs.

**English poster — seed 43 · DiT FP8 · PSNR 14.40 dB**

> A poster with the exact text "QWEN IMAGE 2.1", clean typography on a plain background.

| BF16 | DiT-only FP8 |
|---|---|
| [![BF16 English poster, seed 43](../figures/t2i_english_s43/bf16.png)](../figures/t2i_english_s43/bf16.png) | [![DiT FP8 English poster, seed 43](../figures/t2i_english_s43/dit_fp8.png)](../figures/t2i_english_s43/dit_fp8.png) |

The requested title is present in BF16 and missing in DiT FP8. The unrequested portrait already appears in BF16. Combined FP8 also loses the title; Text Encoder FP8 retains it ([all configurations](../GALLERY.md#english-text-regression--seed-43)).

**Chinese bookstore sign — seed 44 · DiT FP8 · PSNR 16.28 dB**

> A front-facing bookstore sign with the exact Chinese text "星河书店", clear readable lettering.

| BF16 | DiT-only FP8 |
|---|---|
| [![BF16 Chinese bookstore sign, seed 44](../figures/t2i_chinese_s44/bf16.png)](../figures/t2i_chinese_s44/bf16.png) | [![DiT FP8 Chinese bookstore sign, seed 44](../figures/t2i_chinese_s44/dit_fp8.png)](../figures/t2i_chinese_s44/dit_fp8.png) |

## 4. Conclusion

- DiT FP8 reduces latency and peak memory, with a text-rendering regression in the tested inputs.
- Text Encoder FP8 saves memory without reducing latency here. Combined FP8 provides the largest memory saving, with latency close to DiT-only and the same missing-title case.
